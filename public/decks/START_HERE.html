Why keep the human in the loop?

If AI can write the notes and draft the email, why not let it send the message automatically?
Suggested answer
Because the output can be incomplete, inaccurate or wrong for this client. The adviser owns the relationship, suitability, compliance and the final judgment. Morgan Stanley tells users to always verify AI output, and advisers review and edit Debrief drafts before anything is sent.
Automation handles the repeatable work. AI helps interpret and generate. A human owns the important decision.

A warning story: when automation goes wrong

KNIGHT CAPITAL · US EQUITY TRADING · 1 AUGUST 2012
Knight Capital deployed new code in its automated order router. The deployment was wrong, and an old, unused function woke up when the market opened.
45 minof runaway orders after the open
4M+orders sent to fill 212 customer orders
$460M+loss
Before the open, the system sent 97 automated emails pointing to an error. Nobody acted. What controls were missing?
Suggested answer
The SEC found weak controls on code deployment and testing, no effective limit on total orders and exposure, and no clear procedure for responding to incidents. Knight paid a $12 million penalty. Lesson: automation does not remove risk. It can make one error very large, very fast. Test before release, set hard limits, and make sure a human can stop the system.
Source · U.S. SEC · SEC Charges Knight Capital With Violations of Market Access Rule (16 Oct 2013)

It counts as v0 if…

  • it starts without a developer fixing it first;
  • it accepts at least one simple input;
  • one end-to-end interaction works;
  • it shows one visible output—even if the logic is simple;
  • the team can say what is real, fake and still missing.

Not required today

  • A polished design
  • A trained ML model
  • Production deployment
  • Perfect data

Extra case: Nasdaq market surveillance

ML · ANOMALY DETECTION · INVESTIGATION

The problem

Surveillance teams process huge volumes of trading activity. They must surface possible market abuse without flooding investigators with low-value alerts.

The workflow

Market data→Rules + ML→Prioritised alerts→Investigator

The lesson

AI can help rank and surface cases. It does not remove the need for investigation and judgment.

DISCUSSION

If a model creates fewer alerts but misses one serious case, is it really better?

Suggested answer
Not necessarily. The right metric depends on the cost of each type of error. In high-risk surveillance, catching serious cases may matter more than reducing alert volume. Compare the model with the current process, using metrics linked to the real decision.
